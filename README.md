# AI Chest X-ray TB Screening at Rural Clinics

A proposal to the Lesotho Ministry of Health for an 18-month pilot. Four mobile teams with portable X-ray units and AI reading software would screen **40,000 adults** for tuberculosis at about **20 rural health centres** in two highland districts. Anyone the software flags gives a sputum sample for a confirmatory test the same day.

**Estimated cost:** US$0.69–1.12 million  
**Status:** Proposal, dated 12 November 2026. Awaiting endorsement by the Ministry of Health.

> **Decision requested:** endorse the pilot in principle, name a focal person in the National TB Programme, agree the two districts, and authorise approaches to funders.

## Why this pilot

### The problem

- **Very high TB burden.** Lesotho had 664 TB cases per 100,000 people in 2023. Half of people with TB also have HIV, and 18.5% of adults aged 15–49 live with HIV.
- **Screening is far from where people live.** Without X-ray at local health centres, people must travel to a district hospital for a first test.
- **The tools are approved.** WHO recommends computer-aided detection (CAD) for TB screening in people aged 15 and over, and approved six CAD products in June 2025.
- **It already works in Lesotho.** SolidarMed and Swiss TPH run CAD4TB on a digital X-ray truck at St Charles Hospital, Seboche, serving Butha-Buthe and Mokhotlong.

### Why now

- **Policies validated.** In 2025, more than 100 stakeholders validated Lesotho's AI, Data Management, and Broadband and Shared Infrastructure policies.
- **Strategy under way.** In August 2026, MICSTI and the United Nations began drafting a National AI Strategy. The Deputy Prime Minister asked that safeguards, infrastructure and skills come before AI spending scales up. This pilot is small, staged and gated to meet that test.
- **Prices just fell.** Since July 2026, CAD software costs US$2,500 a year or US$7,500 for three years per X-ray unit, with unlimited scans, training and updates. These prices hold to December 2029 and are about 65% below earlier prices.

## How it works

Mobile teams bring X-ray to the clinic, AI reads it in minutes, and anyone flagged gives a sample the same day. **Clinicians make every diagnosis.**

1. **Fixed weekly rota.** Four teams, each with a radiographer and a screener, visit about five health centres each on a weekly schedule. Battery-powered, backpack-sized X-ray units need no X-ray room.
2. **Result in minutes.** The CAD software scores each X-ray for TB on a laptop, offline. People above the threshold give a sputum sample the same day for a GeneXpert molecular test.
3. **Local threshold.** The cut-off score is set with the National TB Programme using the first 2,000 tested people, balancing missed cases against the number of confirmatory tests.
4. **Who is screened.** Adults 15 and over: household contacts, people living with HIV, ex-miners, and anyone with symptoms. Under-15s follow the usual clinical pathway, because WHO does not yet recommend CAD for them.
5. **Nobody lost after a positive result.** Results link to the national TB register. Village health workers get an SMS to follow up anyone who has not started treatment within 14 days.
6. **Clinicians decide.** CAD flags people for testing; it never diagnoses. Diagnosis needs a confirmatory test.
7. **Learning from what exists.** Teams will learn from the SolidarMed and Swiss TPH work before choosing sites.

## Phasing

The pilot runs for 18 months in three phases. The full screening rota starts only if the pilot passes a review at month 9.

| Phase | Months | Main activities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Set up** | 1–3 | Buy kits and licences; train four teams; agree lab capacity |
| **Pilot** | 4–9 | Screening starts; set the CAD threshold; test SMS follow-up |
| ◆ **Month-9 gate** | 9 | Go only if flagged people are tested and confirmed cases start treatment |
| **Scale** | 10–18 | Full weekly rota; about 20 health centres; 40,000 adults screened |

If the pilot misses at month 9, it stops or is redesigned before more money is spent.

## Risks and mitigations

The main risk is that people flagged by the AI are never tested or treated, so follow-up is built in from day one.

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| People screened positive are not tested or treated | Same-day sputum collection; SMS follow-up by village health workers; tracked weekly |
| Too many false positives overload labs | Local threshold calibration; agree lab capacity with the Ministry of Health before launch |
| Radiation-safety or licensing delays | Low-dose units; licences applied for in month 1; trained operators only |
| Equipment breaks down in rough terrain | Warranty and spare-parts contract; one unit kept as a rotating backup when needed |
| Stigma keeps people away | Screen everyone in a group, not only the sick; work with chiefs and support groups |
| Patient data exposed | Data held in Lesotho; access by role; consent at screening |

## Governance and safeguards

The National TB Programme owns the pilot. A steering group with MICSTI reviews it at month 9.

- **Data protection.** The Data Protection Act 2011 (Act No. 5 of 2012) applies, but its Data Protection Commission has not yet been appointed. The pilot runs its own data protection impact assessment, takes consent at screening and keeps patient data in Lesotho.
- **Radiation safety.** Only trained, licensed operators use the units, under national radiation-safety rules.
- **Clinical oversight.** The National TB Programme sets the CAD threshold and reviews results monthly.

## Prototype: Highland TB Screen

`Highland TB Screen.html` is a single-page demo of the tools the pilot would use. It has three views:

- **Screening station** – register a person by code, record a (simulated) X-ray score against the threshold, mark sputum collection, and get a plain-language explanation in Sesotho or English.
- **Programme dashboard** – the care cascade from screening to treatment, results by site, a follow-up list, and a tool for setting the CAD threshold from calibration records.
- **Protocol** – sample screening protocol passages that the assistant answers questions from.

To try it, download the file and open it in a browser. The AI features (explanations, protocol answers, SMS drafts and questions about the data) work only when the page is opened as a Claude artifact on claude.ai while signed in; elsewhere the page shows "Claude unavailable here".

> **This is a demo with synthetic data.** The X-ray score is simulated; the pilot would use WHO-approved CAD software on the laptop. People are identified by code only, and no real patient data is used. The districts shown (Thaba-Tseka and Mokhotlong) are illustrative. The protocol text was written for the prototype; the pilot will load the National TB Programme's own standard operating procedures. Highland TB Screen is not a medical device and not an official government service.
